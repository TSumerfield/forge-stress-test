# Forge Diagnostic OS

## Purpose
A Forge diagnostic should improve a practitioner's understanding of a real system or decision. It is not a personality quiz, engagement gimmick, or arbitrary score generator.

## Core intelligence schema
Capture six layers where relevant:
1. Context: school/department fingerprint and constraints.
2. Decision/problem: what is actually being decided or diagnosed.
3. Evidence: what is known and evidence strength.
4. Assumptions: what must be true and how well supported it is.
5. External intelligence: comparables and credible external evidence.
6. Follow-up: what happened later and what was learned.

## Decision Stress Test output
Where applicable include: decision as understood; PROCEED / MODIFY / DELAY / REJECT; confidence; decisive issue; what would change the recommendation; assumptions and evidence strength; missing considerations; external intelligence; risks; and a concise recommendation.

## Question design
Every question must earn its place by changing interpretation, segmentation, scoring, recommendation, or future research value. Remove questions that merely feel interesting. Prefer observable behaviour and concrete conditions over vague self-ratings.

## Scoring
Never change scoring solely to make results feel more positive. Scores must have an explainable relationship to the underlying construct. Version scoring changes. Preserve comparability or explicitly mark a break in methodology.

## Output
Give the practitioner insight, not just a number. Explain the strongest signal, biggest constraint, and one useful next action. Avoid false precision.

## Validation
Test diagnostics with ordinary practitioners, realistic data, edge cases, incomplete answers, and contradictory responses. Review whether the result is understandable without Forge explaining it verbally.
