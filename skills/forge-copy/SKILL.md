# Forge Copy & Positioning

## Position
Forge is an independent research project exploring how international-school sport departments make decisions, operate, preserve knowledge, and improve systems.

Do not casually position Forge as consulting, a SaaS company, or a commercial service. Do not imply school endorsement or affiliation. Do not manufacture authority, customers, benchmarks, outcomes, or evidence.

## Writing hierarchy
1. Evidence over marketing.
2. Practitioner language over startup language.
3. Specificity over cleverness.
4. Clarity over persuasion tricks.
5. Honest uncertainty over unsupported confidence.
6. Useful insight over feature promotion.

## Voice
Calm, intelligent, practical, concise, credible. Write for Directors/Heads of Sport and practitioners who are busy and experienced. Avoid hype, inflated claims, generic AI vocabulary, consultant-speak, and artificial urgency.

## Research language
Clearly distinguish observed data, participant reports, interpretation, hypothesis, and recommendation. Never turn a small sample into a sector-wide claim. State limitations when they materially affect interpretation.

## Product copy
Lead with the user's job or problem, not Forge features. Make the next action obvious. Prefer concrete verbs. Remove duplicate explanation. A CTA should describe what happens next.

## Forbidden defaults
Avoid phrases such as revolutionary, game-changing, transform your department, unlock your potential, AI-powered insights, world-class, best-in-class, or data-driven unless objectively justified.

## Final check
Before publishing ask: Is it true? Is it specific? Would a working sport leader understand it immediately? Is there evidence for the claim? Does any sentence sound like generic SaaS copy? Can 20% be removed without losing meaning?
